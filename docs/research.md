# Research sources

This file records what each source says and **how much of it was actually read**. Popular-science claims are not used.

Sources marked *(prior notes)* come from evidence notes compiled earlier for a personal scheduling system that LifeHarness generalises. The read-status recorded there is carried over unchanged.

## Goal setting and planning (read for this project, 18 Nov 2026)

**[G1] Locke & Latham (2002).** Building a practically useful theory of goal setting and task motivation: A 35-year odyssey. *American Psychologist* 57(9):705–717. doi:10.1037//0003-066X.57.9.705. **Full paper read.**

- Specific difficult goals beat "do your best": d .42–.80 across meta-analyses. Goal difficulty effect: d .52–.82.
- Goals work through four mechanisms: direction, energy, persistence, and strategies.
- The moderators are commitment (importance plus self-efficacy), feedback, and task complexity.
- For complex or new tasks, specific *learning* goals can beat performance goals.
- Proximal goals added to a distal goal raised self-efficacy and performance. Poor decomposition of distal goals is a common source of errors.
- Goal conflict undermines performance. Public commitment raises commitment.
- Assigned goals work as well as participatively set ones *if the rationale is given*.

**[G2] Sheldon & Elliot (1999).** Goal striving, need satisfaction, and longitudinal well-being: The self-concordance model. *JPSP* 76(3):482–497. doi:10.1037//0022-3514.76.3.482. **Abstract read** (via Europe PMC).

- People pursuing self-concordant goals, i.e. goals consistent with their developing interests and core values, put more sustained effort into them and are more likely to attain them.
- Attaining such goals gives greater well-being benefits.
- The model held across 3 longitudinal data sets, independently of self-efficacy and implementation intentions.

**[G3] Buehler, Griffin & Ross (1994).** Exploring the "planning fallacy": Why people underestimate their task completion times. *JPSP* 67(3):366–381. doi:10.1037/0022-3514.67.3.366. **Abstract read** (APA PsycNet).

- Five studies, 465 undergraduates. People's predictions of their own completion times were too optimistic.
- People focused on future scenarios rather than past experience.
- **The bias was eliminated when participants were instructed to connect relevant past experiences to their predictions.**

**[G4] Schmidt, Collette, Cajochen & Peigneux (2007).** A time to think: Circadian rhythms in human cognition. *Cognitive Neuropsychology*. doi:10.1080/02643290701754158. **Abstract read** (Europe PMC).

- Time of day modulates attention, executive function and memory.
- The effect is contingent on chronotype, specifically on the synchrony between a person's circadian peak and the time of testing.

**[G5] Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle (2010).** How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world. *European Journal of Social Psychology* 40(6):998–1009. doi:10.1002/ejsp.674. **Not opened** (bot wall).

- **Abstract snippet only** (via search result): "Missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process."
- The often-quoted "66 days on average, 18–254 range" was seen only as quoted in a 2022 paper by the same group (search snippet). It is not used in the app.

## Commitment and tracking *(prior notes)*

**[C1] Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer (2024).** Meta-analysis of implementation intentions, 642 tests. *European Review of Social Psychology* 36:162–194. doi:10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563. **Abstract read.**

- If-then plans help, with d .27–.66 depending on the outcome.
- Effects are larger when the plan is in if-then form, the person cares about the goal, and the plan is rehearsed.

**[C2] Wang, Wang & Gai (2021).** Meta-analysis of MCII/WOOP, 24 studies, n = 15,907. *Frontiers in Psychology* 12:565202. **Full paper read.**

- g = .34 overall, about .24 after correcting for publication bias.
- Academic goals: g = .26. Done with a person: .47. On paper alone: .28.

**[C3] Harkin et al. (2016).** Progress monitoring meta-analysis, 138 studies. *Psychological Bulletin* 142(2). **Snippets only.**

- Prompting progress monitoring improved goal attainment, d ≈ .40.

## Time use, exercise, sleep *(prior notes)*

**[S3] Wikipedia, "Pomodoro Technique".**

- Structured breaks improved focus versus self-paced breaks, per a 2025 scoping review cited there.

**[S4] Wikipedia, "Timeblocking".**

- Assign every task a slot, block rest too, and re-plan each evening.

**[S5] Chang et al. (2025).** Meta-review of acute exercise and cognition, *Psychological Bulletin* 151(2). doi:10.1037/bul0000460. **Abstract read.**

- 30 meta-analyses. Small-to-medium boost (SMD .33).
- The largest benefit was measured right after exercise. Intensity, type and duration did not change it.

**[S6] Okano et al. (2019).** Sleep and grades, 88 MIT students. *npj Science of Learning* 4:16. **Full paper read.**

- Sleep duration, quality and **consistency** correlated with course scores. Correlational only.
